## The sign rule

Do the numbers without signs first. Then look at the signs:

| Signs | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| + and + | + |
| − and − | + |
| + and − | − |
| − and + | − |

**Same signs give +. Different signs give −.** Division follows exactly the same rule.

## Examples

- (−3) × 4: 3 × 4 = 12, different signs → **−12**.
- (−3) × (−4): 12, same signs → **12**.
- (−20) ÷ 5: 20 ÷ 5 = 4, different signs → **−4**.
- (−20) ÷ (−5): 4, same signs → **4**.

## Three numbers

Count the minus signs. **Even** number of minus signs → **+**. **Odd** → **−**.

- (−2) × (−3) × 4: 2 × 3 × 4 = 24, two minus signs (even) → **24**.
- (−2) × (−3) × (−4): 24, three minus signs (odd) → **−24**.

## Why does − × − give +?

A pattern: (−3) × 2 = −6, (−3) × 1 = −3, (−3) × 0 = 0. Each step goes up by 3. Keep going: (−3) × (−1) = **3**.

## World — children's rights

Reading time: about **11 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

**Today, November 20, is World Children's Day.** On **November 20, 1989**, the United Nations adopted the **Convention on the Rights of the Child**.

### What it says

A "child" is anyone **under 18**. Every child has rights, including:

- **to go to school**,
- **to be heard** on things that affect them,
- to health care, safety from harm and from dangerous work, play, and a name and nationality.

Almost every country has ratified it, which makes it the most widely ratified human rights treaty in history. The only UN member that hasn't is **the United States**, which signed it in 1995 but never ratified it.

### Young people who stood up

- **Malala Yousafzai**, from **Pakistan**, blogged about girls' right to go to school. In 2012, at 15, she was shot. She survived, kept speaking, and in **2014**, at **17**, became the youngest **Nobel Peace Prize** winner ever.
- **Iqbal Masih**, also from **Pakistan**, was forced to work in a carpet factory as a small child. He escaped at 10 and spoke around the world against child labor, before he was killed at 12.
- **March for Our Lives, 2018:** after a school shooting in Florida, US students organized one of the biggest youth protests in American history.
