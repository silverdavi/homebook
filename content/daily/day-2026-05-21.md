**Today's new things: inverse + fraction division.**

Both of these are short rules. The hard work was already done — once
you have multiplication, division is essentially free.

## Inverse — the easiest rule of the week

The inverse of a fraction is the fraction **flipped**.

- Inverse of **3/5** is **5/3**.
- Inverse of **2/7** is **7/2**.

For an integer, treat it as "n/1" and flip it:

- Inverse of **7** is **1/7**.
- Inverse of **9** is **1/9**.

That's it. There's no calculation. You don't even multiply anything.

## Division — multiply by the inverse

Dividing by a fraction is the same as multiplying by its inverse.

> **a/b ÷ c/d  =  a/b × d/c**

Worked example: **2/3 ÷ 4/5**

1. Flip the second one: 4/5 → 5/4.
2. Now multiply: 2/3 × 5/4.
3. Top: 2 × 5 = 10. Bottom: 3 × 4 = 12. 10/12 = **5/6**.

That's the whole technique. Three steps: **flip the second one,
multiply, reduce**.

## Periodic — row 3 finishes

| Z | Symbol | Element    | Most common N | Electrons |
|---|--------|------------|---------------|-----------|
| 15 | P     | Phosphorus | 16            | 15        |
| 16 | S     | Sulfur     | 16            | 16        |
| 17 | Cl    | Chlorine   | 18            | 17        |
| 18 | Ar    | Argon      | **22**        | 18        |

**Argon is the trap.** Atomic number 18, but its most common isotope
has 22 neutrons (40Ar). Don't reflexively say "18 = 18 = 18" — that's
the right answer for protons and electrons, but wrong for neutrons.
