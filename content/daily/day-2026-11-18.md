**A triangle is half a rectangle: base × height ÷ 2.**

Read this first (about **8 minutes**). It builds on Day 34 (area of a rectangle) and Day 44 (volume).

## Half a rectangle

Draw a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm. Its area is 6 × 4 = **24 cm²**. Cut it corner to corner. You get **2 equal triangles**, each **12 cm²**.

That works for every triangle:

**Area of a triangle = base × height ÷ 2**

## What "height" means

The **height** goes straight up from the base to the top corner, at a right angle. It is **not** the slanted side.

## Examples

- Base 10 cm, height 7 cm: 10 × 7 = 70, then ÷ 2 = **35 cm²**.
- A 8 × 5 rectangle cut corner to corner: 40 ÷ 2 = **20 cm²** each.
- Area 30 cm², base 12 cm: double the area (60), divide by the base: 60 ÷ 12 = **5 cm** tall.

| Want | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Area | base × height ÷ 2 |
| Height | area × 2 ÷ base |
| Base | area × 2 ÷ height |

## World — apartheid and Nelson Mandela

Reading time: about **11 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

### What apartheid was

In **South Africa**, from 1948 to the early 1990s, **apartheid** ("apartness") was a set of **laws that split people by race and gave white people power**. Black South Africans, most of the country, couldn't vote, live where they chose, or go to the same schools or beaches.

### Resistance

- **Nelson Mandela** helped lead the African National Congress. In 1962 he was jailed, and he spent **27 years** in prison, 18 of them on **Robben Island**, off Cape Town.
- **Soweto, 1976:** students in Soweto, next to **Johannesburg**, protested being **forced to learn in Afrikaans**, the rulers' language. Police shot at them. 13-year-old Hector Pieterson was killed, and his photo became a symbol of the struggle.
- Around the world, people used **boycotts**: refusing to buy South African goods, play its sports teams, or invest money there. US students pushed their universities to pull out their money.

### Freedom

In 1990, Mandela walked out of prison without seeking revenge. In **1994**, in the first election where everyone could vote, he became **South Africa's first Black president**.

Instead of only punishing people, the country held the **Truth and Reconciliation Commission**, led by Archbishop Desmond Tutu. **Victims told their stories, and some abusers confessed** in public.
