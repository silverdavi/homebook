**Prices go up and down by percents. Find the change, then add it or take it away.**

Read this first (about **8 minutes**). It uses the percent anchors from Day 31: 10% is one tenth, 25% is one quarter, 50% is half.

## Going up

**Example:** a $60 bike goes up 25%.

1. Find the change: 25% of $60 = $60 ÷ 4 = **$15**.
2. Add it: $60 + $15 = **$75**.

## Going down: discounts

**Example:** a $80 jacket is 10% off.

1. Find the change: 10% of $80 = **$8**.
2. Take it away: $80 − $8 = **$72**.

## Finding the percent

**Example:** a price went from $40 to $50.

1. The change: $50 − $40 = **$10**.
2. Divide by the **old** price: $10 ÷ $40 = 1/4 = **25%**.

Always divide by where you **started**, not where you ended.

| Question | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Goes up p% | find p% of the price, then add |
| p% off | find p% of the price, then subtract |
| What percent? | change ÷ old price |

## World — climate change and who pays

Reading time: about **11 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

### What's happening

Some gases in the air trap heat, like a blanket around Earth. That's the **greenhouse effect**, and some of it keeps Earth livable. But since the **Industrial Revolution**, when factories and engines started **burning coal** (and later oil and gas), we've added a lot of **carbon dioxide**, the main warming gas. The blanket got thicker, and Earth has warmed about 1.2°C.

**Weather** is one day. **Climate** is the average over many years. One cold day doesn't disprove warming.

### Who caused it

- **Most each year now:** **China**.
- **Most in total since 1850:** **the United States**. Per person, Americans still emit about twice as much as people in China.
- Many poor countries together emitted almost nothing.

### Who gets hurt

- **Tuvalu** and other low island nations: most of Tuvalu is less than 3 meters above the sea. Rising seas can flood it.
- **Pakistan, 2022:** floods covered about a third of the country. Pakistan causes under 1% of world emissions.

This is **climate justice**: the people who caused the least harm often suffer the most.

### What the world agreed

- **2015, Paris Agreement:** almost every country agreed to keep warming **well below 2°C, aiming for 1.5°C**. Each country writes its own plan.
- **2022, COP27:** after decades of pushing by poor countries, a **"loss and damage" fund** to help countries hit by climate disasters.
- **2018:** **Greta Thunberg**, age 15, began a school strike outside Sweden's parliament. In 2019, millions of students around the world joined.

### What works

**Solar panels** and **wind turbines** make electricity without burning fuel, and they're now the cheapest new electricity in most of the world. Better buses and trains, electric cars, and insulated homes help too.
