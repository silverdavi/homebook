**Distance = speed × time. Cover the one you want, and the other two tell you what to do.**

Read this first (about **8 minutes**). It uses the units lesson from Day 36: km per hour is a rate.

## Speed is a rate

**60 km/h** means 60 kilometers **per** hour: every hour, you go 60 km.

## The triangle

Draw a triangle. Put **D** (distance) on top, **S** (speed) and **T** (time) on the bottom.

- Want **D**? Cover it: S and T are side by side, so **multiply**: D = S × T.
- Want **S**? Cover it: D is over T, so **divide**: S = D ÷ T.
- Want **T**? Cover it: D is over S, so **divide**: T = D ÷ S.

## Examples

- A bus goes 50 km/h for 3 hours: 50 × 3 = **150 km**.
- A train goes 240 km in 4 hours: 240 ÷ 4 = **60 km/h**.
- A car drives 180 km at 60 km/h: 180 ÷ 60 = **3 hours**.

| Want | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Distance | speed × time |
| Speed | distance ÷ time |
| Time | distance ÷ speed |

## World — refugees: why people flee

Reading time: about **11 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

### What a refugee is

A **refugee** is someone who **fled their country because of war, danger, or persecution and can't safely go back**. A **migrant** may move for work or family. Refugees have special legal protection because going home isn't safe.

**Asylum** is **protection a country gives to someone fleeing danger**. Asking for it is a legal right.

### Why the rules exist

During World War II, many countries turned Jewish refugees away. In 1939, the ship *St. Louis* was sent back to Europe with over 900 Jewish refugees, and many later died in the Holocaust. **Albert Einstein** was one of the lucky ones: he fled Nazi Germany in 1933.

After the war, millions in Europe had no safe home. So in **1951**, countries signed the **Refugee Convention**: they promised **not to send refugees back to danger**.

### Today

The UN refugee agency (UNHCR) counted about **123 million** people forced from their homes in 2024, **over 100 million**, more than ever.

- **Syria:** a civil war since 2011. **Turkey** has hosted over 3 million Syrians, more than any other country.
- **Ukraine:** Russia invaded in 2022, and millions fled to Poland, Germany, and beyond.
- **Sudan:** a war since April 2023 forced over 12 million people from their homes, the world's largest displacement crisis.

### Who takes them in

Most refugees **don't** go to rich countries. About 70% stay in **poorer countries next door** to the one they fled.
