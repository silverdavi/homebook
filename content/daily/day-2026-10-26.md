**Units tell you what to do: multiply, divide, or flip.**

Last week you found density by dividing. Today answers the bigger question: *how do you know when to multiply and when to divide?* Start with the animation above (about **6 minutes**). Then read this (about **8 minutes**).

## 1. Units are part of the number

**4 cm** is not just 4. The **cm** comes along, and it behaves like a number:

- cm × cm = **cm²** (a flat square: area)
- cm × cm × cm = **cm³** (a little cube: volume)

A box 4 cm long, 3 cm wide, and 2 cm tall:

**4 cm × 3 cm × 2 cm = 24 cm³**

That is 2 layers of 12 little cubes. Volume is how many 1 cm cubes fill the box.

## 2. "Per" means divide

The word **per** means "for each one." It is written with a slash: km**/**h, $**/**kg, g**/**cm³.

A train goes 120 km in 2 hours. How far in **each one** hour?

**120 km ÷ 2 h = 60 km/h**

Whenever the answer has "per" in it, you divide.

## 3. Multiply to use a "per"

A bike goes 15 km/h for 3 hours. How far?

**15 km/h × 3 h = 45 km**

The hours are on the bottom of km/h and on top in 3 h, so they **cancel**, like 3 ÷ 3. Only **km** is left, which is exactly what was asked.

## 4. Flip when you want the bottom

Rice costs $3 per kg. You have $12. How many **kg**?

kg is on the **bottom** of $/kg. To get the bottom, **divide by the rate**:

**$12 ÷ $3/kg = 4 kg**

Same with density: 40 g at 8 g/cm³ → 40 ÷ 8 = **5 cm³**.

## The one check that always works

Before you calculate, ask: **which units do I want left over?** Then pick the operation that leaves exactly those units.

| You want… | You have… | Do this |
| --- | --- | --- |
| something **per** something | two amounts | **divide** (top ÷ bottom) |
| the **top** unit (km, $, g) | a rate and the bottom unit | **multiply** |
| the **bottom** unit (h, kg, cm³) | an amount and a rate | **divide by the rate** |

If you get a strange unit like **cm³/g** or **km/h²**, you picked the wrong operation. The units warned you.

## Extra — only if you finished early (optional)

Nothing here is on the exam. It is for when the page went fast and you want to know more.

- **Converting is multiplying by 1.** 100 cm = 1 m, so (100 cm ÷ 1 m) is just 1. Then 3 m × (100 cm ÷ 1 m) = **300 cm**; the m cancels.
- **Area units grow fast.** 1 m = 100 cm, but 1 m² = 100 × 100 = **10,000 cm²**, and 1 m³ = **1,000,000 cm³**. Draw it if that sounds wrong.
- **Challenge:** how many seconds are in one day? Write it with units so they cancel: 1 day × (24 h / 1 day) × (60 min / 1 h) × (60 s / 1 min).
- **Real scientists:** in 1999, NASA lost the $125 million Mars Climate Orbiter because one team used pound-seconds and another used newton-seconds. Nobody checked the units.

## World — four current crises, and how they got here

Reading time: about **14 minutes**. These notes are current as of **October 2026**. Find each place on a map before you read about it. (This was planned for Friday; Friday was a day off.)

### Ukraine — Eastern Europe

- **Where:** north of the Black Sea, bordered by Russia to the east and Poland to the west.
- **Background:** Ukraine was part of the Russian Empire and then the Soviet Union. Under Stalin, the famine of 1932–33 (the **Holodomor**) killed millions of Ukrainians. Ukraine became independent in **1991**.
- **2014:** Russia seized Crimea and backed fighting in eastern Ukraine.
- **2022:** Russia launched its **full-scale invasion**. Attacks continue to kill civilians and damage homes, power, water, schools, and hospitals.

### Sudan — northeastern Africa

- **Where:** south of Egypt, along the Nile and the Red Sea.
- **Background:** Britain and Egypt ruled Sudan until 1956 and governed the north and south very differently. That helped lead to decades of civil war, and **South Sudan** became a separate country in 2011.
- **2023:** war broke out between the **Sudanese Armed Forces** and the **Rapid Support Forces**, a paramilitary group that grew out of the militias accused of atrocities in Darfur in the 2000s.
- **Today:** Sudan is the **world's largest hunger and displacement crisis**. More than 10 million people have been forced from their homes.

### Gaza — on the eastern Mediterranean Sea

- **Where:** a narrow strip of land, about 40 km long, between Israel and Egypt on the **Mediterranean Sea**. About 2 million people live there.
- **Background:** Britain ruled Palestine from 1920 to 1948. During the 1948 war that followed the creation of Israel, about 700,000 Palestinians fled or were driven out; Palestinians call this the **Nakba** ("catastrophe"). Many refugee families ended up in Gaza. Israel and Egypt have tightly restricted Gaza's borders since 2007.
- **Today:** a 2025 ceasefire improved some access, but most people still need shelter and food assistance. **Funding shortages** are cutting the aid.

### Myanmar — Southeast Asia

- **Where:** between India, Bangladesh, China, and Thailand.
- **Background:** Myanmar was a British colony until 1948. In **2017**, the military drove more than 700,000 **Rohingya** Muslims into Bangladesh. In **2021**, the military overthrew the elected government.
- **Today:** civil war, forced displacement, hunger, and climate disasters overlap.

### Why colonial history matters

In Sudan, Gaza, and Myanmar, colonial rulers **shaped the borders, institutions, inequalities, and political power** that people live with today. Colonialism doesn't explain everything automatically, and people today make their own choices. But you can't understand these conflicts without it.

### How to think about any conflict

- **People are not their governments.** Civilians do not automatically share every action of a government or armed group. A flag does not tell you what each person believes.
- **Compare credible sources.** Governments and armed groups tell competing stories, but that doesn't make every claim equally supported. Social-media posts are not equal evidence.
- **One consistent rule:** protect civilians, let humanitarian aid through, and oppose collective punishment, no matter which side is doing it.
