# Tuesday, November 10 — Day 45

**An equation is a balance. Do the opposite to both sides, and x is left alone.**

Read this first (about **8 minutes**).

## What x means

**x** is a number we don't know yet. In x + 4 = 10, x is the number that makes both sides equal. You might already see it's 6. Today you learn a method that works even when you can't see it.

## Undo it

Every operation has an **opposite** that undoes it:

| If x has… | Undo it by… |
| --- | --- |
| + 4 | subtracting 4 |
| − 4 | adding 4 |
| × 4 (written 4x) | dividing by 4 |
| ÷ 4 | multiplying by 4 |

Whatever you do to one side, **do to the other side too**, so it stays balanced.

## Four examples

- x + 4 = 10 → subtract 4 from both sides → **x = 6**.
- x − 3 = 8 → add 3 to both sides → **x = 11**.
- 5x = 35 → divide both sides by 5 → **x = 7**.
- x ÷ 2 = 9 → multiply both sides by 2 → **x = 18**.

**Check by putting it back:** 6 + 4 = 10. ✓

## A word problem

Tickets cost $6 each. x tickets cost $42. That's **6x = 42**. Divide both sides by 6: **x = 7 tickets**.

## World — what a fair wage is

Reading time: about **11 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

### The minimum wage

The **US federal minimum wage** is **$7.25** an hour. It was last raised in **2009**. Prices have gone up since then, so $7.25 **buys less every year**. That's inflation.

States can set their own, as long as it's **at least the federal one**. Washington state is over $16. About 20 states still use $7.25.

A **living wage** is pay high enough to cover **rent, food, and basics** where you live. In most US cities, it's far above $7.25.

### Fight for $15

In **2012**, about 200 **fast-food workers in New York City** walked off the job and asked for $15 an hour and a union. People laughed. Since then, many states and cities have raised their wages to $15 or more.

A **labor union** is workers who **join together to bargain** with their employer. Together, they can win better pay and safety than one person alone.

### Who makes your clothes

Many of the world's clothes are sewn in **Bangladesh**, where many garment workers earn about $100 a month.

In **2013**, the **Rana Plaza** factory building collapsed near Dhaka. Cracks had been found the day before, but workers were ordered back in. **1,134** people died. Afterward, over 200 clothing brands signed the **Bangladesh Accord** to pay for **factory safety checks**, and workers won the right to refuse unsafe work.

### Children at work

About **140 million** children worldwide work instead of only going to school, most on farms. A **Fair Trade** label on coffee or chocolate means the farmers were paid a **fair minimum price**, with no child labor.
