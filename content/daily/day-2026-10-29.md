**Two kinds of average: the mean and the median.**

Start with the “Two kinds of average” animation above (about **6 minutes**). Then read this (about **6 minutes**).

## The mean: add, then divide

The mean is what everyone would get if you shared everything out equally.

Mean of 2, 7, 4, 9, 3:

1. Add: 2 + 7 + 4 + 9 + 3 = **25**
2. Divide by how many numbers: 25 ÷ 5 = **5**

## The median: the middle

1. Put the numbers **in order**: 2, 3, 4, 7, 9
2. The **middle** one is the median: **4**

Today every median question has an odd count (3 or 5 numbers), so there is always exactly one middle.

Don't forget step 1. The median of 8, 2, 6, 4, 10 is not 6 because 6 is in the middle spot; it's 6 because in order (2, 4, **6**, 8, 10) it's in the middle.

## Why have two?

Four people earn $40,000 and one earns $10,000,000.

- The **mean** is over $2 million, which describes **nobody** in the room.
- The **median** is **$40,000**, which describes a typical person.

One giant number pulls the mean but barely moves the median. That's why news about pay or house prices usually reports the **median**.

## Extra — only if you finished early (optional)

- **Even count:** for 2, 4, 6, 8 there are two middles (4 and 6). The median is halfway between them: **5**.
- **The mode** is the number that appears most often. In 3, 5, 5, 7, 9 the mode is 5. Shoe stores care about the mode.
- **Try it:** time yourself running to the corner 5 times, then find your mean and median times. Which one is better if one run you tripped?

## World — cities, people, and the fight for fair work

Reading time: about **12 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

### 8 billion of us

In **November 2022**, the UN marked the day world population passed **8 billion**. In 1800 it was about 1 billion.

- **India** has had the **largest population** since **2023**, when it passed **China**; each has over 1.4 billion people.
- Around **2007**, for the first time in history, **more than half** of all people lived in cities. Today it's about 57%.

### Giant cities

- Asia has many of the biggest: **Tokyo, Delhi, Shanghai, Jakarta, and Dhaka** each have well over 20 million people in their metro areas.
- Africa's cities are growing fastest. **Lagos, Nigeria** is a center of music (Afrobeats), film ("Nollywood"), and tech. **Kinshasa**, the capital of the **Democratic Republic of the Congo**, is the largest French-speaking city in the world.
- About **1 billion** city dwellers live in informal settlements, often without safe water, toilets, or legal ownership of their homes.

### How cities got so big: the Industrial Revolution

Around **1760**, the **Industrial Revolution** began in **Britain**. Steam engines and factories made cloth, iron, and goods faster than ever, and millions moved from farms **to find work in the new factories**.

Life in factory cities was harsh: 12–16-hour days, dangerous machines, and children as young as 6 at work.

### Workers fought back

Workers organized **labor unions** and went on strike. Over decades they won:

- the **8-hour day** ("eight hours for work, eight hours for rest, eight hours for what we will")
- the **weekend**
- limits on **child labor**

In **1911**, the **Triangle Shirtwaist Factory** in New York caught fire. The doors were locked to stop breaks, and **146 garment workers**, mostly young Jewish and Italian immigrant women, died. Public anger and union pressure led to **new workplace safety laws** that still protect workers today.

### Median, again

Economists report **median income** because **a few very rich people pull the mean up**. If you want to know how a typical worker is doing, ask about the median.
