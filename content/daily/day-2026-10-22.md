# Thursday, October 22 — Day 35

**Density tells you how tightly matter is packed.**

This is a new subject. The first block teaches it before any independent work. This reading takes about **8 minutes**.

## Start with one rule

**density = mass ÷ volume**

- **mass** is how much matter, measured here in grams
- **volume** is how much space, measured here in cm³
- **density** is the mass packed into each cm³

## Find density

A block has a mass of 24 g and a volume of 6 cm³.

24 ÷ 6 = **4 g/cm³**

Read that answer as “4 grams in each cubic centimeter.”

## Find mass

After density is comfortable, use:

**mass = density × volume**

If density is 4 g/cm³ and volume is 5 cm³:

4 × 5 = **20 g**

Today does not ask you to rearrange all 3 formulas at once. Practice density first, then mass. The exam uses those same two forms.

## World block — flags, borders, and migration

This part takes about **11 minutes**.

- **Ukraine:** blue over yellow.
- **Israel:** blue and white with a Star of David.
- **Palestine:** black, white, and green stripes with a red triangle.
- **South Africa:** six colors and a sideways green Y.

A **state** is a political territory with a government. A **nation** is a community connected by identity, culture, language, or history. They overlap, but they are not always the same.

People migrate for family, work, education, climate, safety, and many other reasons. A **refugee** is someone forced across a border by persecution, war, or violence. An asylum seeker asks another country for legal protection.

Borders are not timeless. Empire, colonization, war, treaties, and negotiation have all moved them. Studying a border should include the people whose lives it divides, not only the governments that claim it.
