**Integers extend the number line below zero.**

Start with the “Through zero” animation above (about **5 minutes**). Then read this (about **6 minutes**).

## Start with direction

- Positive numbers are to the right of zero.
- Negative numbers are to the left of zero.
- Adding a positive number moves right.
- Subtracting a positive number moves left.
- Adding a negative number also moves left: 9 + (−13) is the same trip as 9 − 13 = **−4**.

## Cross zero one step at a time

Start at −3 and add 8. Move 8 spaces right:

−3 + 8 = **5**

Start at 5 and subtract 12. Move 12 spaces left:

5 − 12 = **−7**

## Temperature is the same number line

It is −4°F, then the temperature rises 9 degrees:

−4 + 9 = **5°F**

It is 5°F, then the temperature falls 7 degrees:

5 − 7 = **−2°F**

The minus sign belongs to the number. Write −7, not 7. Today uses only number-line movement and temperature changes.

## World — water: rivers, rights, and who controls them

Reading time: about **12 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here.

### The water cycle

**Heat from the sun** evaporates water from oceans, lakes, and leaves. The vapor rises, cools into clouds, and falls as rain or snow. It flows downhill in rivers back to the sea, and then it starts again. The water you drink today may once have been rain on a dinosaur.

### How much water can we use?

About **97%** of Earth's water is salty ocean. Only about **3%** is fresh, and most of that is frozen in glaciers and ice sheets. The rivers and lakes people drink from are a tiny slice.

A **watershed** is all the land that drains into the same river or lake. Rain on a parking lot miles away can end up in your town's drinking water, which is why pollution anywhere in a watershed matters.

### Great rivers

- **The Nile** is the longest river in Africa, about 6,650 km. It flows north through **Ethiopia, Sudan, and Egypt** to the Mediterranean. Almost all Egyptians live close to it; without the Nile, Egypt would be desert.
- **The Amazon** (South America) is not the longest, but it carries the **most water** of any river: about a fifth of all river water reaching the oceans.
- **The Mekong** flows through **Southeast Asia**: China, Myanmar, Laos, Thailand, Cambodia, and Vietnam. Tens of millions of people depend on its fish and rice fields.

### Who controls the water?

Water flows downhill, and it doesn't stop at borders. Whoever is **upstream** can build dams and hold it back.

- **The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam** gives Ethiopia electricity it badly needs. But **Egypt and Sudan depend on the Nile water flowing downstream**, so the dam has caused years of tense negotiations.
- **The Aral Sea** in Central Asia was once one of the world's largest lakes. Starting in the 1960s, Soviet planners diverted its rivers to grow cotton. It **shrank to about a tenth of its size**, leaving fishing boats stranded in a dusty desert.

### Water and justice

- In **2010**, the UN General Assembly recognized safe drinking water and sanitation as a **human right**. About 2 billion people still lack safely managed drinking water.
- In **2014**, officials in **Flint, Michigan**, a majority-Black city run at the time by a state-appointed manager, switched its water source to save money. The new water corroded old pipes and released **lead**, which harms children's brains. Residents complained for over a year before officials admitted the problem.
- In **2016**, the **Standing Rock Sioux Tribe** and thousands of allies protested the Dakota Access oil pipeline, which runs under the lake that supplies the tribe's water. Their slogan was *Mni Wiconi*, "water is life."
