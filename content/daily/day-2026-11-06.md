# Friday, November 6 — Day 43

**Probability is how likely something is, from 0% (never) to 100% (always).**

Read this first (about **8 minutes**).

## Chance as "out of"

A bag has 3 red marbles and 7 blue. Pick one without looking.

- There are 3 + 7 = **10** marbles, and **3** are red.
- The chance of red is **3 out of 10**, or 3/10.
- As a percent: 3 out of 10 = 30 out of 100 = **30%**.

Always count **all** the marbles first. The chance of red is red ÷ total, not red ÷ blue.

## What to expect

A fair 6-sided die gives each number a **1 in 6** chance. Roll it 60 times, and you **expect** about 60 ÷ 6 = **10** ones.

"Expect" means **about**. You might get 8 or 13. The more you roll, the closer you usually get.

## Spinners

A spinner has 5 equal parts, and 2 are red. Spin it 50 times.

1. One part: 50 ÷ 5 = **10** spins.
2. Two red parts: 2 × 10 = **20** red spins expected.

| Question | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| % chance | count the wins and the total, then make it out of 100 |
| Die, how many? | rolls ÷ sides |
| Spinner, how many? | spins ÷ parts, then × red parts |

## World — Native nations today

Reading time: about **11 minutes**. Every World question on the exam comes from here. November is **Native American Heritage Month** (since 1990).

### Nations, not just history

There are **574** federally recognized Native nations in the US. Each is **sovereign**: it has its own **government, laws, and courts**. That comes from **treaties**: written agreements between nations. The US signed about 370 treaties with Native nations, and broke many of them.

The largest by land is the **Navajo Nation**, across **Arizona, New Mexico, and Utah**. At about 27,000 square miles, it's bigger than 10 US states.

### Removal

The **Indian Removal Act of 1830** let the government force Native nations off their land in the East. On the **Trail of Tears**, the Cherokee were forced to march to what is now Oklahoma, and thousands died. The Muscogee (Creek), Seminole, Chickasaw, and Choctaw were removed too.

Native Americans born in the US only all became **citizens in 1924**, and some states blocked them from voting into the 1950s.

### Fighting back, and winning

- **Standing Rock, 2016:** the Standing Rock Sioux, in **North and South Dakota**, led protests against the Dakota Access oil pipeline, which threatened their water and sacred sites. Thousands of people joined. The cry was **"Mni Wiconi": water is life.**
- **McGirt v. Oklahoma, 2020:** the Supreme Court ruled that much of eastern Oklahoma is **still Muscogee (Creek) land** under an 1830s treaty. Justice Gorsuch wrote: "we hold the government to its word."
